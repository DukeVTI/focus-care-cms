 Focus Monthly Report / Utilization This document outlines the technical requirements for developing an automated Reporting &amp; Analytics Engine within the FocusCMS platform. The goal is to aggregate data from the Chronology and Daily Observation modules to generate person-centered, Ofsted-compliant reports. Project Overview Objective: Create a module that synthesizes raw daily logs into structured Weekly/Monthly reports. Tone of Voice: Direct address to the Young Person (YP) (e.g., "You did great at college this week...") Compliance Framework: Ofsted Quality Standards for Children’s Homes. 1. Data Aggregation &amp; Logic The developer must query the database for all entries related to a specific YP_ID within a defined date_range . Utilization Logic: * Calculate "Placement Utilization" by scanning the Daily Observation/Chronology logs for a specific boolean flag or status (e.g., Slept_Onsite: true ). Output: A percentage and a raw count (e.g., "28/31 nights spent at home"). Risk Trend Analysis: Compare the current period’s risk scores (Average/Peak) against the previous period. Logic: If Current_Period_Risk_Avg &gt; Previous_Period_Risk_Avg , flag as "Increased Risk." Task/Goal Tracking: Fetch Tasks where Status = 'Pending' from the previous report. Fetch Tasks where Status = 'Completed' during the current period. Generate a "Status Update" section for each carried-over task. 2. Report Structure (The Schema) The report generation must map data points to the following Ofsted-aligned categories: The Progress You’ve Made (The Care Planning Standard): General overview of the month. Your Health &amp; Wellbeing (The Health &amp; Wellbeing Standard): Aggregated logs from medical appointments, mood trackers, and exercise. Your Education &amp; Learning (The Enjoyment &amp; Achievement Standard): Attendance data and school/college feedback. Your Safety (The Protection of Children Standard): Comments on risk levels, incidents (if any), and how the YP stayed safe. Your Voice (The Leadership &amp; Management Standard): Direct quotes or "YP Views" captured in daily logs. 3. Data Visualization Points The dashboard view of the report should include the following UI components: Risk Heatmap: A line chart showing daily risk levels over the month to identify "trigger days." Mood/Sentiment Wheel: A pie chart aggregating the "Daily Mood" emojis or tags selected in observations. Utilization Gauge: A circular progress bar showing nights slept onsite vs. away. Activity Breakdown: A bar chart showing categories of activities (e.g., Education vs. Social vs. Life Skills). 4. Developer Instructions: The "Letter" Generator The system must use a template engine (like Handlebars or a custom JSON-to-Text parser) to ensure the tone is correct. Instruction: Avoid clinical "The service user..." language. Use the variable {{YP_FirstName}} and "You." Example Template Logic: "Hi {{YP_FirstName}} , looking at your last month, you stayed with us for {{utilization_count}} nights. We noticed your risk level regarding {{risk_type}} was lower than last month—well done on making safer choices!" 5. Task Management Integration Previous Tasks: The UI must display: "Last month, we agreed you would [Task]. You [Achieved/Did not achieve] this because [Link to Log Entry]." New Tasks: A form field at the end of the report generation process to set 3 SMART goals for the next period, which will then auto-populate the "Previous Tasks" section of the following month’s report. 6. Technical Requirements Export Formats: PDF (formatted for printing/sharing with Social Workers) and an on-platform "YP Portal" view. Audit Trail: Every report generated must be timestamped and linked to the staff member who "finalized" the automated draft. Triggers: Allow staff to set a "Report Due" reminder every 7 or 30 days. The following instructions focus on the automation logic and temporal data processing required to handle monthly, quarterly, and multi-year summaries. Technical Instructions for Developers 1. Automated Trigger Engine (The "Cron" Layer) The system must allow administrators to set "Report Triggers." Monthly: Trigger on the 1st of the month at 00:01 (scanning previous_month ). Custom Windows: On-demand triggers for 3, 12, or 24-month summaries. Worker Service: Use a background job runner (like BullMQ or Sidekiq ) to ensure that generating 50 reports simultaneously doesn't crash the dashboard. 2. Data Pre-Processing &amp; "Context Window" Management Since 24 months of daily logs will exceed the token limit of most AI models, you must implement a tiered summarization logic : Daily Tier: AI summarizes daily observations into a weekly "digest." Monthly Tier: The AI uses the weekly digests to create the monthly report. Annual Tier: The AI uses the monthly reports to create the 12/24-month summary. This ensures the "Voice of the Young Person" isn't lost in a sea of data. 3. Risk &amp; Utilization Aggregation (Visuals) The AI must not "guess" the numbers. The developers must build a data-pipe that feeds specific JSON objects to the AI for the following: Utilization: Total_Nights_In_Period vs Nights_Logged_Onsite . Risk Delta: (Current_Risk_Score - Previous_Risk_Score) / Previous_Risk_Score . Task Success Rate: Percentage of Tasks_Assigned vs Tasks_Completed . 4. AI Prompt Engineering Schema The developer must use a structured system prompt to ensure the "Young Person Centered" tone remains consistent across different timeframes. Developer Prompt Logic: "You are an empathetic Social Worker. Using the provided JSON data for {{YP_Name}} , write a letter to them. Timeframe: {{Report_Period}} . Constraint 1: Mention if their nights spent at home (utilization) went up or down. Constraint 2: Be specific about tasks from the previous period. If they didn't achieve a task, be encouraging, not clinical. Constraint 3: Compare risk levels. If risks are lower, celebrate their growth." 5. Reporting Intervals &amp; Categories The platform must support "Comparative Analysis" for longer durations: Report Type Data Focus Visual Requirement Monthly Granular daily progress. Daily Mood/Risk Heatmap. Quarterly (3mo) Trend patterns &amp; habit formation. Comparison bars vs. previous quarter. Annual (12/24mo) Holistic growth &amp; milestone achievement. Multi-year "Journey Map" line graph. 6. Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Workflow Even though it is "automatic," the system must not publish these reports to the YP or Social Worker without a "Staff Review" step. System: Generates draft at 00:01 on the 1st. Notification: Sends alert to the Key Worker: "The Monthly Report for [Name] is ready for review." Action: Staff can edit the AI text, approve the charts, and then click "Publish to YP Portal." This logic flow ensures that no task "falls through the cracks." It requires a relational database structure where tasks are linked across reporting periods. Developer Instruction: Recursive Task Tracking Logic To ensure the AI accurately comments on previous goals and sets new ones, the system must follow a "Chain of Responsibility" pattern for task data. 1. The Data Retrieval Pipeline When the report generation triggers (e.g., the 1st of the month), the script must execute a three-step query: Step A (The Past): Query the database for all tasks where Report_ID equals the previous period. Step B (The Present): Cross-reference those Task IDs with Daily_Observation_Logs to see if a "Task Completed" event was flagged by staff during the current month. Step C (The Future): Create a placeholder for "New Tasks" that will be carried into the next reporting cycle. 2. Logic Flow Diagram for AI Processing Code snippet graph TD A[Trigger: Generate Report] --&gt; B[Fetch Tasks from Previous Report] B --&gt; C{Was Task Completed?} C -- Yes --&gt; D[AI Action: Write a 'Celebration' sentence] C -- No --&gt; E[AI Action: Write an 'Encouragement' sentence + Carry Forward] D --&gt; F[Fetch Current Month Observation Data] E --&gt; F F --&gt; G[Staff Input: Set 3 New SMART Goals] G --&gt; H[Finalize Report &amp; Save Task States for Next Month] 3. AI Prompt Construction (The "Task" Module) The developer should feed the AI a JSON object specifically for tasks. The instruction to the LLM should look like this: System Input (JSON): JSON "previous_tasks": [ { "task_desc": "Attend 3 gym sessions a week", "status": "Incomplete", "reason_found_in_logs": "Injured ankle in week 2", "linked_standard": "Health &amp; Wellbeing" }, { "task_desc": "Complete college application", "status": "Achieved", "completion_date": "2026-01-12", "linked_standard": "Enjoyment &amp; Achievement" } ] AI Instruction: "Review the previous_tasks . For 'Achieved' tasks, congratulate the young person. For 'Incomplete' tasks, look at the reason_found_in_logs . If a reason exists, acknowledge the barrier (e.g., the injury) and explain that this task will stay on the list for next month so we can try again together." 4. Automated Risk-Level Commentary Logic The system must calculate a Risk Delta . This is a critical data point for the AI to determine its tone. Logic: $$(Current Month Avg Risk) - (Previous Month Avg Risk) = Delta$$ If Delta is Negative (-): AI tone is Positive/Reinforcing ("We've seen you managing your emotions so much better this month, your safety scores are trending down!"). If Delta is Positive (+): AI tone is Concerned/Supportive ("We noticed things have been a bit tougher for you lately. Your safety logs show more incidents than last month. Let's look at how we can support you more."). 5. Implementation Requirements for the Developer Task Persistence: Tasks must have a Parent_Task_ID so the system can track a single goal across multiple months (e.g., if a 12-month summary is run, the system can show a "Task Progression" graph). Utilization Counter: The "Nights Slept Onsite" must be pulled from the Night_Check or Bed_Log table and presented as a Utilization Gauge in the report header. Final Review UI: Provide a "Task Toggle" in the Staff Dashboard. If the AI incorrectly identifies a task as incomplete, the staff member must be able to manually override the status before the report is sent to the young person.
